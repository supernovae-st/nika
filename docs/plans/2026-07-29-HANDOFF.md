## §1 · THE TWO LAWS · they are the whole point

Both are in `crates/nika-check/src/lib.rs` (module doc) and `AGENTS.md` (hard
rules). Everything below is an application of one of them.

### Law 1 · Cover the claim, or narrow the claim to what you cover

A rung's sentence is a promise. A green that means less than it says spends the
reader's trust and returns nothing. When you cannot widen the coverage, narrow
the sentence and NAME what defers — that option is always available and always
correct.

### Law 2 · An undecidable question almost always contains a decidable one

> When a proof obligation is waived as undecidable, NAME the decision procedure
> it would have needed. If you can write it down, the waiver is a hole.

Four instances, three rungs, three independent authors — one of them hid a
shipped fail-open. It recurs because a true impossibility result FEELS like a
complete answer, so the search stops. **The prompt that finds these is not "be
careful", it is:**

> *What is the strongest claim I CAN decide here, and does the code make it?*

The operative rule, which a reviewer can apply without being an expert: a
comment that waives a check must name the sub-question it considered and why
that one does not survive either. A waiver with no named alternative is
reviewable as incomplete.

## §7 · WHAT THIS ARC SAYS ABOUT THE PRODUCT

Asked directly whether Nika is functional, the honest answer is **yes**, and
the distinction matters: **not one of the 23 defects was an EXECUTION defect.**
Every one was in the JUDGMENT layer — the engine did what it said, but said
more than it knew.

That layer is the differentiator, so finding bugs there is finding bugs in the
thing that makes Nika worth having. The reframe that holds: Kubernetes, Deno
and Docker have shipped this same class of CVE. The difference is not that they
lack the bug — **it is that they have no gate that could find it.** Nika had
one, mis-wired; it is wired now.

What changed is the definition of ready. Before: 1.0 is ready when the features
are there. After: **1.0 is ready when the oracle runs over the corpus and finds
no contradiction.** Harder, slower, and the only criterion that makes the
promise defensible.

The unknown is honest and unresolved: **the yield curve.** Run 2 of the
adversarial audit, on a new domain, is what answers it. That is §3.4, and it is
the most valuable single thing left on this list.
