# Google Flow — Ready to Paste (Hacker Documentary Style)

> Each prompt = 1 clip (~8 seconds). Paste directly into flow.google
> Mode: Video x2. Raw text only — no markdown, no headers.
> Tone: Mr Robot meets @0xSero. Real world. Not anime.

## CLIP 1 — "The Wall" (opening)

```
A close-up of a developer's hands hovering motionless over a keyboard at 3am. The only light comes from multiple monitors casting cold blue light on their face. The screens show cascading error messages, a browser tab reading "API Access: $20/month", and a pricing page with "Enterprise: Contact Sales" highlighted. The camera slowly pulls back to reveal a cramped apartment, empty coffee cups, cables everywhere. The developer exhales, closes the laptop, and stares at the dark ceiling. Style: David Fincher clinical precision, desaturated, shallow depth of field, 35mm film grain, cold blue monitor light as the only source. Real apartment, not a tech office.

"There has to be a better way."

Audio: Mechanical keyboard clatter slowing to silence. Computer fan hum. A frustrated exhale. The distant sound of a city that never sleeps. Complete quiet after the laptop closes.
```

## CLIP 2 — "The Newspeak"

```
A slow tracking shot moves through a split world. On the left side of frame: a gleaming corporate office, AI dashboards glowing on massive screens, executives reviewing generated reports, everything chrome and glass and abundant. On the right side, separated by a visible wall of frosted glass: a small business owner at a cluttered desk, manually typing into a search bar, handwriting notes on paper, no AI tools visible. The camera pushes through the glass wall, which cracks as it passes. The crack spreads like a web across the entire partition. Style: Split diopter shot like Brian De Palma, one side warm and golden, one side cold fluorescent. Corporate documentary aesthetic. Handheld subtle movement.

Audio: A calm narrating voice: "In 1984, they controlled the words. In 2026, they control the tokens." Corporate lobby muzak on the left bleeding into office silence on the right. The glass cracking sounds sharp and final.
```

## CLIP 3 — "The Garage"

```
A tight close-up of hands typing on a mechanical keyboard in a dark room. The only light is a terminal with a dark background and soft blue text. Line by line, clean YAML code appears on screen: "schema: nika/workflow@0.12", then "tasks:", then "- id: liberate", then "infer:" — each line glowing as it's typed. The camera is steady, intimate, focused on the craft. This is not a hacker movie cliche — this is a person building something real, alone, at night, because they believe it matters. The terminal reflects in their glasses. Style: Wong Kar-wai intimacy meets documentary realism. Close, warm despite the darkness. The screen is the campfire. 35mm, shallow focus on the terminal text.

Audio: Cherry MX mechanical key switches — each keystroke precise and satisfying. No music. Just typing. The hum of a single machine. A distant train passing. The last keystroke hits enter, and a single soft chime confirms the file is saved.
```

## CLIP 4 — "The Numbers"

```
A minimalist black screen. White monospace text appears one line at a time, clinical and undeniable, like evidence being presented in court. "LangChain: 5,706 MB peak RAM." Beat. "Nika: 1,046 MB." Beat. "LangGraph: 10,155ms latency." Beat. "Nika: 5,714ms." Beat. "CrewAI: excluded. 44% failure rate." Long beat. "Nika: deterministic." The numbers glow brighter as each comparison lands. The final line appears larger: "The memory advantage is 5x. It's structural." The camera very slowly pushes in on the text throughout. Style: Apple keynote minimalism meets courtroom evidence display. Pure black, pure white text, zero decoration. The data IS the design.

Audio: A low, barely perceptible bass pulse that gets slightly louder with each number reveal. No narration — the numbers speak. A deep, resonant tone when "5x structural" appears, like a gavel hitting.
```

## CLIP 5 — "The Door"

```
A medium shot of a terminal screen in a dark room. A cursor blinks. Then hands type: "nika run morning-briefing.nika.yaml" and press enter. Immediately, lines of output cascade — task names lighting up, parallel execution indicators showing multiple tasks running simultaneously, progress bars advancing in sync. The text flows like water finding its path. The camera slowly widens to reveal the room brightening — not from the screen, but from dawn light creeping through a window behind the developer. The orange sunrise light mixes with the blue terminal light on their face. They lean back and watch it work. Style: The Social Network coding montage pacing. Real terminal output, real dawn. The transition from artificial light to natural light is the metaphor — from locked systems to open ones.

Audio: A rapid-fire cascade of soft completion chimes as tasks finish — like a pinball machine made of crystal. Keyboard sounds, then silence as the workflow runs itself. Birds beginning to sing outside. The sounds of morning replacing the sounds of machines.
```

## CLIP 6 — "The Butterfly"

```
Extreme close-up of a dark terminal screen. In the bottom right corner, a single tiny blue pixel appears. It pulses once, softly. Then unfolds into the silhouette of a small butterfly — just a few pixels, barely visible. It sits still for a moment, then takes flight across the screen, leaving a faint trail of blue light. Where it passes, terminal text transforms — error messages become clean output, "ACCESS DENIED" becomes "workflow completed", dollar signs dissolve into open brackets. The butterfly reaches the edge of the screen and splits into two. Then four. Then eight. The camera pulls back to reveal dozens of blue butterfly silhouettes spreading across multiple monitors, each one transforming locked systems into open ones. Style: Tron Legacy digital aesthetic but subtle and earned. This is the ONLY fantastical element in the series — it arrives after five clips of hard reality. Minimal, geometric, pixel-art butterfly forms. Not ornate — simple.

Audio: A single crystalline note when the first pixel appears. Soft electronic tones as the butterfly moves — not a melody, just harmonics. Each split creates a quiet harmonic overtone. The sound builds from silence to a gentle, shimmering chord that feels like possibility.
```

## CLIP 7 — "The Network"

```
A time-lapse view of a world map at night, seen from slightly above, like a mission control display. Points of light appear one by one across continents — each one a developer running their first Nika workflow. Tokyo. Berlin. Sao Paulo. Lagos. Montreal. Each point pulses when it activates, then sends thin blue lines connecting to nearby points. The network grows organically — not a corporate graph, but like mycelium spreading through soil, or neurons forming connections. Some nodes glow brighter as they become hubs. A counter in the corner ticks up: contributors, workflows run, lines of YAML written by the community. The camera slowly zooms in on one bright node — revealing it's just a laptop screen in a small apartment, glowing blue. Style: Documentary data visualization meets NASA mission control. Real geography, real cities. The network is organic, not corporate. No logos, no branding — just people connecting through code.

Audio: A gentle ambient electronic track building slowly — each new node adds a subtle tone to the harmony. Keyboard sounds from around the world layering into a collective rhythm. The sound of a global community forming, like an orchestra tuning up before the first note.
```

## CLIP 8 — "Liberate Your AI" (closer)

```
A black screen. A terminal cursor blinks three times in the center. Then white monospace text types itself out, letter by letter: "nika run your-future.nika.yaml". The cursor blinks twice more at the end of the line. Long pause — three full seconds of just the cursor blinking on black. Then, very small, below the command, a single line fades in: "Automate AI. No code required." Another beat. The cursor disappears. In its place, the faintest suggestion of a blue butterfly silhouette appears for exactly one second, then fades to pure black. Nothing else. No logo. No URL. Just the command and the silence. Style: Maximum restraint. Pure black (#002b36). Pure white text (#f8fafc). Monospace font. The butterfly is barely there — a whisper, not a shout. This is confidence. This is a tool that doesn't need to sell itself. It just exists, waiting to be run.

Audio: The soft click of each letter appearing, like a typewriter at distance. Silence during the pause. Then a single whispered voice — calm, certain, not dramatic: "Liberate your AI." One crystalline piano note that rings and slowly fades into absolute silence.
```

## Narrative Arc

```
The Wall (problem) → The Newspeak (why it matters) → The Garage (a builder alone)
→ The Numbers (the proof) → The Door (it works) → The Butterfly (hope spreads)
→ The Network (community) → Liberate Your AI (call to action)
```

## Google Flow Tips

- Paste the text between ``` (without the ```)
- Mode: Video x2 for 2 variants
- If prompt is too long, cut after "Style:" line
- Use "Video Extension" to chain clips
- For the butterfly: add a simple blue butterfly pixel art as "Ingredient" (tag: style)
- CLIP 4 and CLIP 8 work best with maximum simplicity — don't add ingredients
